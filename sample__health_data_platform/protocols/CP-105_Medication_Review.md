# Medication Review After Discharge

**Document ID:** CP-105 · **Version:** 1.6 · **Owner:** Pharmacy Services · **Effective:** 2026-02-20
**SAMPLE DOCUMENT — fictional, created for a product demo. Not clinical guidance.**

## Purpose
Medication confusion is one of the most common causes of avoidable readmissions. This protocol makes sure every discharged patient's medication list is accurate and understood.

## Who this applies to
- All patients in the High or Medium readmission risk tier (see CP-104).
- Any patient taking 10 or more medications, or any medication from a high-risk group: blood thinners, insulin, diuretics, or opioids.

## Steps
1. **Compare medication lists within 72 hours** of discharge: what the patient took before admission, what was given in the hospital, and what is on the discharge list. Resolve any differences with the prescriber.
2. **Pharmacist review** for patients taking 10 or more medications or any high-risk medication.
3. **Confirm prescriptions are filled.** Ask the patient to read the labels of new medications during the call.
4. **Teach back.** Ask the patient to explain, in their own words, what each new or changed medication is for and when to take it.
5. **Check for barriers:** cost, pharmacy access, difficulty swallowing pills, or confusion about stopped medications.

## When to escalate
Contact the prescriber the same day if:
- A medication on the discharge list conflicts with one the patient is still taking at home.
- The patient has not started a new high-risk medication.
- The patient reports side effects such as dizziness, bleeding, or very low blood sugar.

## Documentation
Record the reconciled medication list, pharmacist involvement, and any changes in the patient's record and the patient engagement platform.

## Measures of success
- Percentage of High and Medium tier patients with a medication review within 72 hours (target: 90%).
- Number of medication discrepancies found and resolved.
