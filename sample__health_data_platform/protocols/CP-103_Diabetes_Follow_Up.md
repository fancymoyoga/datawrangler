# Diabetes Follow-Up Protocol After Hospital Discharge

**Document ID:** CP-103 · **Version:** 1.2 · **Owner:** Endocrinology and Primary Care · **Effective:** 2026-03-10
**SAMPLE DOCUMENT — fictional, created for a product demo. Not clinical guidance.**

## Purpose
Prevent avoidable return visits for patients with diabetes by making sure blood sugar is monitored, medication changes are understood, and the patient is connected to ongoing care.

## Who this applies to
- Adult patients with diabetes who were admitted for any reason, especially if their diabetes medications changed during the stay.
- **High** risk patients get every step below. **Medium** risk patients get steps 1, 2 and 4.

## Steps after discharge
1. **Medication review within 72 hours** (see CP-105), with attention to any change in insulin or other diabetes medications.
2. **Blood sugar monitoring plan.** The patient knows how often to check, what their target range is, and what to do if readings are too high or too low.
3. **Low blood sugar education.** The patient and caregiver can recognize symptoms and know how to treat them.
4. **Primary care or endocrinology visit within 14 days.**
5. **Diabetes education referral** for patients who are newly diagnosed or newly started on insulin.

## When to escalate
Escalate to the primary care team the same day if:
- The patient reports repeated low blood sugar readings or symptoms.
- Readings stay well above the target range for more than two days.
- The patient has not filled insulin or other diabetes prescriptions.
- The patient cannot be reached after two attempts (follow CP-104, step 4).

## Documentation
Record the monitoring plan, education completed, and outreach outcomes in the patient engagement platform.

## Measures of success
- Percentage of patients with a visit within 14 days (target: 75%).
- Percentage of patients with a documented monitoring plan (target: 95%).
- 30-day readmission rate for patients with diabetes.
